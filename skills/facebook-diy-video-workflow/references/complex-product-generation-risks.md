# Complex Product Generation Risks

Use this operational module before generating product images or video when the product has complex topology or exact functional relationships.

## High-Risk Signals

- Multiple thin cables, cords, wires, branches, loops, or crossing paths.
- Several exact endpoints, plugs, sockets, ports, hinges, clips, or attachment points.
- Many repeated small parts, such as clothes clips, hooks, teeth, rings, or fasteners.
- Dense edges, open frames, grids, lattices, transparent parts, or overlapping structures.
- A correct use method that depends on exact routing, connection order, contact points, or force direction.
- A product whose usefulness becomes false when one part count, connection, or perspective relationship changes.

## Preflight Check

1. Decide whether the topology-critical action should be generated at all. Default to avoiding full disassembly, assembly, threading, routing, or multi-part transformation shots when structural accuracy matters.
2. On company work, recommend explaining the structural risk, likely reroll count, schedule impact, and generation cost to the manager or project lead before production. Propose a shot conversion that preserves the communication goal.
3. Do not treat a completed storyboard, grid image, or correct first frame as proof that video generation is feasible. Temporal generation must preserve every path, part, connection, contact point, and perspective relationship across all frames and may still fail after repeated rerolls.
4. If generation remains mandatory, record the exact part count, cable path, branch topology, endpoints, connector direction, repeated-part pattern, product scale, and correct use state from the references.
5. Inspect every generated first frame at full size before creating video. Compare all critical structural facts against the source.
6. Reject the frame if it invents, removes, merges, duplicates, reconnects, bends, or relocates any critical part, or if perspective makes the product physically impossible.
7. Do not send a structurally incorrect frame into image-to-video. Motion generation usually preserves or amplifies the defect.

## Fallback Strategy

1. Convert the complex process into a verified finished-result shot with voice-over instead of showing the complete transformation.
2. Use a presenter holding the product, packaging, atmosphere, or a non-critical product view when it can communicate the same point without showing exact topology.
3. Keep topology-critical operations in real close-up footage, a real product photo, or a manually corrected composite. Request only minimal camera or environmental motion.
4. If an operation must be generated, split it into units that show only one local area, one connector, one cable branch, one clip group, or one simple action at a time.
5. Reduce simultaneous hand actions, product motion, and camera motion. Keep the product static when structural accuracy is the main task.
6. If structural drift persists in video even after the still image passes, stop rerolling and switch to real footage, a subtle push on a static image, or an editorial transition.
7. Do not imply that a successful storyboard or grid lowers the video-stage risk. Reserve time and budget for rerolls whenever generation is still required.

## Required Feasibility Note

Before proceeding, report: risk level, whether the complex shot should be avoided, the recommended shot conversion, the topology facts that must remain exact, shots AI can safely generate, shots that require a real source, the likely reroll burden, and the selected fallback.
